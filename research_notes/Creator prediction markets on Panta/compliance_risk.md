## Q1. What is the regulatory status of prediction markets as of 2025–2026 (US, UK/EU, key emerging markets), and which jurisdictions do Polymarket and similar platforms geoblock?

### Takeaway
Real-money prediction markets are legal at scale in only one major market, the US, and only through CFTC-registered exchanges (DCMs). Even there, sports and "single-person conduct" contracts are being litigated and restricted by states, and as of late September 2026 two federal appeals courts had sided with the states. Almost every other jurisdiction the app might target treats crypto prediction markets as unlicensed gambling, and enforcement has escalated sharply in 2026: ISP blocks in France, India, Brazil, Hungary and Portugal; fines in the Netherlands; criminal penalties for *promoters* in India and France. Nigeria and Kenya are gray zones with no rule specific to prediction markets, but both have strict advertising and influencer rules. An unlicensed, crypto-settled, creator-promoted app has no clean real-money launch jurisdiction among the ones named in the brief.

### Cited Findings

**United States: federal (CFTC)**
- In 2022 the CFTC fined Polymarket $1.4M and issued a cease-and-desist order for operating an unregistered derivatives platform. This is the template for how the CFTC treats offshore crypto event-contract venues that serve US persons. — [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/regulation/polymarket-seeks-full-cftc-blessing-for-its-on-chain-exchange-report); [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/polymarket-us-regulatory-approval-cftc/)
- The CFTC withdrew its 2024 event-contracts proposal in February 2026. On 16 March 2026 it issued guidance and an ANPRM asking how prediction markets should be regulated, including the manipulation and insider-trading risks "where outcomes may be influenced by a single person or small group." — [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/03/us-cftc-issues-guidance-advance-notice-of-proposed-rulemaking); [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en-us/knowledge/publications/fed865b0/cftc-advances-regulatory-framework-for-prediction-markets)
- On 10 June 2026 the CFTC proposed "Prediction Markets; Public Interest Determinations," amending Rule 40.11 (Federal Register, 12 June 2026). It would replace categorical bans with contract-specific public-interest determinations. One market-integrity factor is whether a contract creates incentives to misuse material nonpublic information. Comments closed 27 July 2026, and I found no final rule as of early October 2026. — [CFTC Federal Register notice](https://www.cftc.gov/LawRegulation/FederalRegister/proposedrules/2026-05105.html); [CRS LSB11441](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11441); [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/cftc-proposes-comprehensive-framework-for-public-interest-review); [Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/6/cftc-proposes-new-rules-for-events-contracts-on-prediction-markets); [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/06/rewriting-the-rulebook-cftc-proposes-rule-changes-for-prediction-market-contracts)
- The proposal stops short of hard bans on sports wagers but says contracts on player injuries and officiating are "likely" impermissible. — [Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/business/sports-betting/2026/cftc-prediction-market-rule-proposal-explain-1234902902/)
- Critics argue the proposal lets agency inaction function as approval of contracts. — [Lawfare](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/the-cftc-is-tying-its-own-hands-on-prediction-markets)
- **CFTC Staff Advisory 26-27 (22 September 2026) on "mention markets"** covers event contracts that settle on the discrete conduct of a named individual: what they say, whether they attend, whom they interact with. Staff wrote that such conduct may be "neither independently generated nor externally verifiable." The advisory treats these contracts as carrying elevated manipulation risk, because settlement can be controlled by the individual, a small group, or people with influence over the individual. Its concern is "not confined" to single-person scenarios. Staff view "independent verifiability and substantial public scrutiny as essential attributes" for listing such contracts under Core Principle 3, and they encourage exchanges to consult staff early. The advisory is not a ban but sets "heightened scrutiny." — [CFTC Letter 26-27](https://www.cftc.gov/csl/26-27/download); [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/our-insights/blogs/viewpoints/102o2yy/cftc-issues-advisory-on-mention-market-event-contracts/); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/379030/cftc-staff-advisory-says-prediction-market-mention-contracts-invite-manipulation); [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/cftc-warns-prediction-markets-over-mention-contracts/)
- **Insider-trading enforcement is live.**
  - The Enforcement Division advisory of 25 February 2026 applies CEA §6(c)(1) and Rule 180.1 to event contracts. It encourages exchanges to use restricted or insider participant lists modeled on sports-league integrity programs. — [CFTC press release 9185-26](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9185-26); [Parker Poe](https://www.parkerpoe.com/news/2026/04/cftc-highlights-enforcement-focus-on-insider-trading-in)
  - April 2026 brought the first CFTC insider-trading charge involving event contracts, against a service member. — [Dentons](https://www.dentons.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/april/27/doj-and-cftc-bring-first-of-its-kind-prediction-market-insider-trading-case); [Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/05/insider-predicting-cftcs-first-action-and-how-we-got-here/)
  - In May 2026 DOJ charged a Google engineer, with a parallel CFTC civil action. — [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/financial-services-law-advisor/2026/05/doj-cftc-case-employee-prediction-market-trading)
  - The advisory cites two enforcement examples. A presidential teleprompter operator (Gabriel Perez) traded "mention" contracts on nonpublic speech information, leading to about $107.5K disgorgement, a $65K civil penalty and a 3-year ban (search summaries of the 28 August 2026 consent order). George Santos was fined $35,000 for manipulating an attendance contract. — [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/cftc-warns-prediction-markets-over-mention-contracts/); [DWT July 2026](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/financial-services-law-advisor/2026/07/prediction-markets-insider-trading-enforcement)
  - The Enforcement Director called the idea that insider-trading law doesn't apply to prediction markets a "myth." — [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/legal/2026/cftc-says-combating-insider-trading-in-prediction-markets-is-a-priority)

### Inferences
- A creator app that lets fans in the US trade real-money YES/NO contracts with stablecoins, without operating through a DCM or FCM, would sit squarely in the fact pattern of the 2022 Polymarket order. This holds regardless of what Panta itself is licensed for, and it should be confirmed with Panta.
- If Panta (like DFlow/Kalshi) passes geoblocking duties to integrators, **the app team, not Panta, carries the geofencing compliance burden.**
- 2026 enforcement aims as much at **promoters and payment rails** as at operators: India criminalizes promotion, France fines advertising, Kenya bans influencer endorsements, and Brazil and India use ISP blocks. For a creator-led model, that means the creators themselves carry personal legal exposure in several target markets, and this should shape creator onboarding and promotion tooling.
- Regulators consistently reject "it's a financial product / trading, not betting" framing (UKGC, Ksa, Brazil's CMN). Relabeling is not a viable mitigation outside the US DCM context.
- The CFTC's own trajectory (Staff Advisory 26-27, the Rule 40.11 proposal) points against listing creator "will I do X" markets even for licensed US venues.

## Q2. What creator-specific risks arise (insider trading/manipulation on creator-controlled outcomes, conflicts in creator resolution, endorsement disclosure, minors in audiences), and what mitigations do others use?

### Takeaway
Creator markets are exactly the "contracts tied to individual conduct" category that the CFTC flagged in September 2026 as presumptively manipulable, and the first creator-insider case (a MrBeast editor fined by Kalshi) is already on the books. The app's core loop is "creator makes a market about themselves, then earns fees and promotes it." That loop stacks three problems: an insider who controls the outcome, an undisclosed material connection, and a young audience. The fix has to be built into the product: ban or demote creator-controlled outcomes, never let creators resolve, put creators and their teams on a restricted-trader list, force #ad disclosure into share flows, and age-gate.

### Cited Findings
- **First creator-insider case.** On 25 February 2026 Kalshi sanctioned MrBeast video editor Artem Kaptur with $20,397.58 in total (about $5.4K disgorgement plus a $15K fine) and a 2-year suspension. The breach was Rule 5.17(y), insiders trading on related markets. Surveillance flagged near-perfect win rates on long-shot markets, other traders reported him too, and he was found to have "failed to cooperate." Kalshi treated him as tied to the "Source Agency" for the MrBeast contracts, which is how creator staff became insiders. Kalshi also reported about 200 insider-trading probes in a year. — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/02/25/nx-s1-5726050/kalshi-insider-trading-enforcement-actions); [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2026/02/25/kalshi-fines-mrbeast-employee-20000-for-insider-trading-related-to-youtube-stream); [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/media-platforms/tv/mrbeast-editor-kalshi-insider-trading/)
- Creator markets attract real money. Traders put hundreds of thousands of dollars on what MrBeast will say in his next video, plus markets on subscriber counts and his marriage date. Beast Industries adopted a "no tolerance" policy for employees and *Beast Games* contestants betting on MrBeast-related topics. — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/02/25/nx-s1-5726050/kalshi-insider-trading-enforcement-actions)
- **Self-betting by the subject.** A California gubernatorial candidate (Kyle Langford) who bet on himself drew a 5-year ban and a fine of about $2.2K. Kalshi later added rules plus an in-market reporting tool for flagging suspected violations. — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/02/25/nx-s1-5726050/kalshi-insider-trading-enforcement-actions); [Fox 13](https://www.fox13news.com/news/prediction-market-kalshi-reveals-new-insider-trading-rules)
- **Regulators on single-person outcomes.** CFTC Staff Advisory 26-27 says settlement on a named person's conduct "may be neither independently generated nor externally verifiable." It also flags people with "access to or influence over the individual," which describes a creator's team, editors and collaborators. ### Inferences (design mitigations)
- **Market taxonomy by who controls the outcome.**
  - Tier A, allowed for real money: externally verifiable events with public resolution sources, such as sports results (where legal), award outcomes, chart positions, or a third-party platform's public metrics with a clear snapshot time.
  - Tier B, play-money or points only, or blocked: outcomes the creator or their circle can influence. "Will I post Friday?", "Will I say X on stream?", "Will I hit 1M subs by date?" (the creator can buy or pump this), "Will my collab happen?"
  - Tier C, always blocked: death, injury, self-harm, violence, minors, war/terrorism, and single-person conduct of private individuals.

  This mirrors CFTC Advisory 26-27, which effectively treats Tier B as needing "independent verifiability and substantial public scrutiny," something most creator self-referential markets lack.
- **Restricted-trader list (the "Source Agency" model).** The creator, their managers, editors, family and collaborators attest at onboarding and are blocked by wallet and KYC identity from trading the creator's markets. Surveillance flags anomalous long-shot win rates, as in the Kaptur case. Add a public "report suspicious trading" button, as Kalshi did.
- **Creators must never be the resolver** of their own markets. Creator fees should not depend on which side wins, and creators should not hold positions. Consider capping creator fees per market so outcome manipulation pays less.
- **Disclosure baked into share flows.** Every share or deep link a creator generates should carry a default, non-removable "#ad / I earn fees from this market" label; for video, an on-screen and spoken disclosure template. Run periodic audits and remove or terminate creators who don't comply, which is the good-faith posture Ifrah recommends.
- **Creator eligibility screening.** Require audience-age analytics before onboarding. In the UK, a creator with 100K+ under-18 followers, or a creator under 25, effectively cannot promote gambling. Block creators whose audience skews under 18. In Kenya and India, creator promotion of real-money markets is illegal outright.

- No data was found on the actual age composition of prediction-market creators' audiences.

## Q3. What do Meta (Instagram/Facebook/WhatsApp), TikTok, X, Apple and Google allow, and could posting market links get creators' accounts restricted? (PWA vs native)

### Takeaway
Every major platform treats real-money prediction markets as gambling, crypto, or a special category that requires licence-backed pre-approval. Apple, Google Play and Google Ads all key access to holding licences "in the locations where the app is used." TikTok's organic Community Guidelines forbid general users from promoting gambling, and X moved in 2026 to bar paid affiliates from posting about gambling. An unlicensed creator app should expect **organic creator posts linking to real-money markets to be at risk of removal or strikes** on TikTok, and paid or affiliate promotion to be blocked on Meta, TikTok and X without licence evidence. Native iOS and Android distribution for real-money trading is effectively unavailable without licences, which pushes toward a PWA. But a PWA does not cure the social-platform and legal risks.

## Q4. How should allowed markets, resolution, disputes, oracles, and anti-wash/sybil controls be designed?

### Takeaway
The best-documented failures in crypto prediction markets are resolution failures, not pricing failures. UMA's token-weighted votes resolved a $7M Ukraine market "Yes" with no deal in place, and flipped a market of $58M–$240M+ on whether Zelensky wore a suit, with fewer than 10 holders supplying nearly all the votes. On-chain volume metrics are also heavily gamed: Columbia researchers estimate about 25% of Polymarket volume over three years was wash trading. Creator markets are smaller, more subjective and easier to influence, so they need stricter rules than Polymarket's: objective named sources, fixed snapshot times, no creator resolvers, bonded challenge windows, and Sybil-resistant incentives.

### Cited Findings
- **Ukraine mineral deal (March 2025).** A $7M market "Ukraine agrees to Trump mineral deal before April?" went from 9% to 100% and resolved Yes without a deal. One UMA whale holding about 5M UMA across three accounts reportedly cast about 25% of the votes. Polymarket refused refunds: "because this wasn't a market failure, we are not able to issue refunds." — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/348171/polymarket-says-governance-attack-by-uma-whale-to-hijack-a-bets-resolution-is-unprecedented); [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/defi/polymarket-s-usd7m-ukraine-mineral-deal-debacle-traced-to-oracle-whale); [Web3 Is Going Great](https://web3isgoinggreat.com/single/polymarket-governance-attack)
- **Zelensky suit (June–July 2025).** The rules relied on a "consensus of credible reporting." After two disputes UMA's DVM resolved No even though BBC and Reuters described the outfit as a suit. Fewer than 10 holders supplied nearly all of the ~18M UMA staked, with three addresses at over 70%. Critics note that penalizing losing voters encourages herding toward the expected majority. UMA's co-founder denied manipulation. — [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/nfts-and-web3/polymarket-controversy-heats-up-after-the-zelenskyy-suit-market-resolves); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/311634/polymarket-allegations-oracle-manipulation)
- **2026:** an $85M Polymarket market on a MicroStrategy bitcoin sale went to a token vote after a dispute. — [KuCoin News](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/85m-polymarket-dispute-over-microstrategy-s-bitcoin-sale-tests-uma-s-oracle)
- **Wash trading (Columbia).** Sirolly, Ma, Kanoria and Sethi (SSRN, not peer-reviewed) estimate that about 25% of Polymarket volume over three years was wash trading. It peaked near 60% in December 2024, fell below 5% by May 2025 and rose to about 20% by October 2025. They flagged 14% of 1.26M wallets and about $4.5B in trades. The enablers they cite are no KYC, no trading fees, and an expected token airdrop. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/347842/columbia-study-25-polymarket-volume-wash-trading); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/11/07/polymarket-wash-trading-inflated-prediction-markets-columbia-research); [Study PDF](https://gamblingharm.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Polymarket-Wash-Trading-Study.pdf)
- **Disallowed categories by others.**
  - A Kalshi speaker says it does not run markets on war, death or terrorism. Kalshi nonetheless faced criticism over a contract tied to the fall of Iran's supreme leader, who died in a US strike. — [KUOW](https://kuow.org/law/2026-08-14/king-county-judge-orders-kalshi-to-stop-most-online-betting-in-washington-state)
  - The CFTC proposal frames enumerated public-interest categories, and contracts on injuries and officiating are "likely" impermissible. — [Sportico](https://www.sportico.com/business/sports-betting/2026/cftc-prediction-market-rule-proposal-explain-1234902902/)
  - Ukraine banned Polymarket over war-related markets. — [AOL/Finance](https://www.aol.com/finance/major-prediction-market-faces-34th-170100115.html)
- **Election markets** triggered the Portugal (presidential election) and Netherlands (Dutch elections) actions, and Brazil's AGU is considering action over 2026 presidential markets. — [World Casino Directory](https://news.worldcasinodirectory.com/portugal-shuts-down-polymarket-amid-suspicious-betting-on-presidential-election-121384); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/358725/netherlands-bans-polymarket-over-illegal-gambling-services); [G3 Newswire](https://g3newswire.com/brazils-attorney-generals-office-considers-action-against-polymarket-over-election-betting/)
- **Surveillance and reporting.** Kalshi detected the creator insider through statistical surveillance and crowd reports, and added a user-facing reporting tool. — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/02/25/nx-s1-5726050/kalshi-insider-trading-enforcement-actions); [Fox 13](https://www.fox13news.com/news/prediction-market-kalshi-reveals-new-insider-trading-rules)

### Inferences (design rules)
- **Market-creation linting (hard requirements before a creator can publish):**
  1. A single named, public resolution source with a URL. Examples: an official league site, the Billboard chart page, a platform's public counter captured by a third-party snapshot service.
  2. A precise resolution timestamp and timezone.
  3. Objective, binary criteria. No "credible reporting consensus" or definitional words like "suit."
  4. A pre-declared fallback for ambiguity: resolve 50/50 or void and refund. Avoid forced Yes/No on ambiguous events, which is what turned the Zelensky case into a scandal.
  5. A category classifier that blocks death, injury, self-harm, violence, war/terror, minors, private individuals, crime, and elections or politics in jurisdictions where those are banned (Brazil, Portugal, Netherlands).
  6. A flag for "creator-controlled outcome," which routes the market to play-money only (see Q2).
- **Resolution pipeline:**
  - The creator proposes a market but never resolves it.
  - Resolution is proposed by the platform or a neutral resolver and posted with evidence, followed by a bonded challenge window (optimistic-oracle pattern) in which challengers post bonds and the loser's bond is slashed.
  - Escalation goes to a multi-party panel (e.g., 3–5 independent resolvers, or a reputable oracle), **not** a token-weighted vote open to whale concentration.
  - Publish the evidence log for every resolution.
  - For price-type markets such as crypto prices, use price oracles (Pyth/Switchboard) with defined feeds and timestamps.
- **Anti-wash and anti-sybil.**
  - A small taker fee, which kills free wash trading.
  - No token or airdrop speculation tied to volume.
  - Leaderboards and referral rewards gated on KYC-unique users and *net* P&L against independent counterparties, not gross volume.
  - Cluster detection on closed counterparty loops (the Columbia method).
  - Referral rewards vest only after organic activity, with caps per creator.
  - A creator-fee basis that excludes self-trades and trades by flagged clusters.

### Gaps
- UMA's 2025 oracle changes for Polymarket (managed proposer whitelists, "MOOV2") and any move to an in-house oracle were not verified because the search budget ran out. From background knowledge, not verified: UMA introduced a managed optimistic oracle restricting who can propose resolutions for Polymarket in 2025.
- Pyth and Switchboard documentation (feed coverage, staleness thresholds, event-resolution products) was not reviewed.
- Panta's resolution mechanism (who resolves, whether creators can resolve) is unknown and is the single most important integrity question for this app.

## Q5. What consumer-protection, responsible-gambling, KYC/age-verification and AML/sanctions controls are expected?

### Takeaway
Every licensed pathway and every platform gate assumes the same baseline: age verification to 18+ or 21+, geo-restriction by licence footprint, risk warnings and responsible-gambling information, and sanctions screening. The Columbia wash-trading study ties Polymarket's integrity problems directly to its lack of KYC, and Polymarket itself was reported moving toward trader KYC in 2026. A wallet-only, no-KYC design is a liability for real money.

### Cited Findings
- **OFAC.** Polymarket's docs put OFAC jurisdictions (Iran, Syria, Cuba, North Korea, Crimea/Donetsk/Luhansk) on full block with no position exit, and Kalshi's agreement covers comprehensively sanctioned territories. DFlow requires builders to block sanctioned territories. — [Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/geoblock); [DFlow](https://pond.dflow.net/legal/prediction-market-compliance)
- **KYC trend.** Polymarket was reported moving toward trader KYC as sanctions and legal risks intensified. — [cryptonews.net](https://cryptonews.net/news/legal/32926181/) (headline-level)
- **No KYC enables wash trading and multi-wallet farming** (Columbia study). — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/347842/columbia-study-25-polymarket-volume-wash-trading)
- **Kenya** names IP geoblocking *and identity verification* as the tools offshore operators must use, with fines up to KSh50M. — [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/economy/kenya-to-fine-foreign-firms-sh50m-for-allowing-kenyans-gamble-5530636)
- **Netherlands.** Ksa investigators simply opened accounts from a Dutch IP and deposited €10. Weak geofencing is how enforcement cases get built. — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/358725/netherlands-bans-polymarket-over-illegal-gambling-services)
- **India.** Users bypass blocks with VPNs, DNS changes and mirror sites, and MeitY has warned VPN providers. — [AGBrief](https://agbrief.com/news/india/18/05/2026/india-warns-vpn-providers-as-kalshi-polymarket-bypass-online-gaming-law/)
- **Platform-mandated RG.** Google Play requires responsible-gambling info and age-gating. TikTok requires "clear warnings about the risks" and mandatory disclaimers. Meta bans under-18 targeting. — [Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9877032); [TikTok Ads](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-gambling-and-games); [Meta](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/gambling-games)
- **Polymarket US** offers only fully collateralized contracts, with no margin or leverage. — [Bitcoin Magazine](https://bitcoinmagazine.com/markets/polymarket-rolls-out-us-app)

### Inferences (design mitigations)
- **Layered geofence:**
  1. IP and geolocation at the PWA edge and at the API, as DFlow requires for both frontend and API.
  2. VPN and proxy detection.
  3. KYC-verified country of residence before any real-money deposit.
  4. Close-only mode for users who move into a restricted region, mirroring Polymarket's tiers.
- **Sanctions.** Screen wallets at connect time and on deposit, using the OFAC SDN digital-currency addresses plus a commercial analytics API. Hard-block OFAC jurisdictions with no exit. Polymarket's full-block tier shows the industry norm.
- **Age.** Run age verification (document plus selfie) before real-money trading. Play-money mode can have lighter gating, but should still exclude under-13s for COPPA reasons and target 18+.
- **Responsible-gambling kit:**
  - Deposit and loss limits, default-on for new users.
  - Cooling-off and self-exclusion that also block the user's linked wallets.
  - A clear display of implied probability, fees and maximum loss on every trade ticket.
  - Session reminders.
  - Promoted "markets you can't afford to lose on" warnings.
  - No credit or margin; stay fully collateralized like Polymarket US.
- **Stablecoin rails.** If the app ever custodies or transmits funds, it may itself be a VASP or money transmitter in some jurisdictions, triggering AML programs and Travel Rule obligations. A non-custodial, Panta-settled design reduces but does not eliminate this; confirm with counsel.

## Q6. Do "opinion market", play-money/points, sweepstakes, or skill-based framings reduce risk, and what are their pitfalls?

### Takeaway
Pure play-money with no cash-out is the only framing with consistent support. It is Manifold's model since March 2025, Meta's planned "Arena" model, and it falls outside Meta's gambling definition, which requires value on *both* entry and prize. Sweepstakes or dual-currency models are being legislated out of existence (California AB 831, in force January 2026) and are banned on TikTok Shop. "Skill" framing fails in India, where the ban covers skill games explicitly. "Trading/financial product" framing has been explicitly rejected by the UK Gambling Commission, the Dutch Ksa and Brazil's CMN, though it does matter for TikTok ad eligibility.

### Inferences
- **Recommended dual-mode architecture.**
  - **"Fan Forecasts" (points mode)** is the global default: no purchase, no cash-out, no transferable token, and no prizes of monetary value. Creator revenue comes from sponsorships or subscriptions, not from trading fees on points.
  - **"Pro Markets" (real-money mode)** is enabled only in jurisdictions with a licensed path, behind KYC, age checks and geofencing.

  This mirrors Manifold and Meta Arena, and keeps the viral creator loop (shareable links, leaderboards) out of the riskiest categories.
- **Pitfalls to avoid in points mode:**
  - Points that can be bought, or that convert to stablecoins, NFTs or tokens. That supplies the "consideration + prize" elements and resembles AB 831-style dual currency.
  - Prize draws for top forecasters. Apple flagged a similar app, and TikTok Shop and many states treat these as sweepstakes.
  - An airdrop tied to points volume, which invites the wash and sybil farming seen at Polymarket.
- "Opinion market" or "poll" labels help only if nothing of value is staked and nothing of value is won.

## Q7 (Synthesis). Risk register with product/design mitigations, and a realistic launch-geography view

### Takeaway
For the Colosseum/Panta hackathon, the credible story is "compliance by design": a points-first creator forecasting product, plus a real-money module that is architected for, but off by default until, a licensed partner and legal opinion exist. Product rules should be built to the strictest emerging standard, CFTC Advisory 26-27 on individual-conduct markets: no creator-controlled outcomes for money, no creator resolvers, restricted-trader lists, and bonded neutral resolution. Real-money launch geography is narrow. None of the named target countries (US, UK, EU, Nigeria, Kenya, India, Brazil) offers a clean path for an unlicensed crypto creator-market app as of October 2026.

### Inferences: launch-geography view (as of October 2026; not legal advice)
- **Do not launch real money (geoblock or close-only):**
  - US (except via a DCM/FCM partner).
  - UK (UKGC licence needed; FCA binary-options ban).
  - France, Netherlands, Portugal, Hungary, Belgium, Switzerland, Poland, Germany and Italy (national bans or blocks).
  - Singapore and Thailand.
  - **India** (criminal for operators, players *and promoters*; ISP blocks).
  - **Brazil** (CMN ban on non-economic event derivatives; Anatel blocks).
  - Australia and the Canadian provinces (Polymarket close-only).
  - All OFAC territories.

- **Gray / high-risk, points-mode only until a local legal opinion and licensed partner exist:**
  - **Nigeria:** no prediction-market rule, but betting is licensed by the NLRC and state boards, the SEC regulates digital assets under ISA 2025, and ARCON requires pre-approval of influencer ads.
  - **Kenya:** regulator in transition; influencer gambling endorsements banned; offshore operators must block residents or face fines up to KSh50M.
  - **Philippines and Indonesia:** not verified here; treat as high-risk.
- **The hackathon path that is credible to judges:**
  - Demo on devnet, or with points.
  - Show the market linter, restricted-trader list, neutral resolution with a bonded challenge, geofence and KYC hooks, and #ad share cards.
  - Present real-money as a jurisdiction-gated module that switches on only with a licensed partner. The DCM/FCM route used by Polymarket US, or Kalshi's tokenized markets via DFlow on Solana, is a precedent: builders inherit the exchange's market rules but must enforce its geoblocks.
- **The creator-fee model itself is a risk multiplier.** Fees tied to trading volume on markets about the creator create both a manipulation incentive and a material connection. Consider flat or sponsorship-based creator compensation in points mode, and capped, outcome-neutral fees in real-money mode.
