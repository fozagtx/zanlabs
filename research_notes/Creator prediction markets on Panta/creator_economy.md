# Creator Economy Fact Base for a Creator-Led, Audience-Based Prediction Market App (Solana / Panta)

Research date: 2026-10-07. Method note: The research used web search only. Search snippets were the main input. WebFetch failed with DNS errors on every primary domain tried (goldmansachs.com, influencermarketinghub.com, chainalysis.com, bcg.com, axios.com, techcabal.com, decrypt.co), and the session's search budget ran out before the last targeted queries. Many figures therefore come from secondary coverage of primary reports, and they are flagged where that matters. Figures are labeled with their year. Older (pre-2025) figures are marked.

## 1. How large is the creator economy (creators, revenue, growth) as of 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
There is no single authoritative size. The most-cited framing is Goldman Sachs' (2023) ~$250B, projected to reach ~$480B by 2027 on ~50M creators. Hard 2025 money flows are better anchors: US creator ad spend of ~$37B (IAB 2025, forecast to reach $44B in 2026), YouTube's >$100B paid out over 2022–2025, OnlyFans' ~$6.3B to creators in FY2025, and Patreon's ~$2B/yr. Creator headcounts range from 50M to 200M+ depending on definition, and they are not comparable.

### Cited Findings
**Market size / growth (global)**
- Goldman Sachs (2023 note by Eric Sheridan): creator economy TAM of ~$250B "today", which could roughly double to ~$480B by 2027. The base is ~50M global creators growing at a 10–20% CAGR over five years. — [Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/the-creator-economy-could-approach-half-a-trillion-dollars-by-2027); dated to 2023 by [FlexOS (Nov 2023)](https://www.flexos.work/learn/who-wants-a-slice-of-the-480-billion-creator-economy-pie). Note: the $480B is a 2027 forecast, not a 2025 measurement.
- Goldman Sachs: only ~4% of global creators are "professionals" earning >$100k/yr, and brand deals are ~70% of creator revenue (survey data). — [Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/the-creator-economy-could-approach-half-a-trillion-dollars-by-2027)
- Goldman's 2025 work (PitchBook-based, covered by Business Insider in Aug 2025) put creator-economy startup funding at ~$1.5B in 2024. It says funding peaked in 2021, slowed in 2022–23, and reaccelerated in 2024. — secondary, surfaced via [FirstMark/Business Insider](https://firstmark.com/story/firstmark-consumer-investors-recognized-by-business-insider/) (I could not verify against the Goldman original)
- An unverified blog attributes 67M creators in 2025, rising to 107M by 2030, to Goldman. I could not confirm this against Goldman's report. — [blog.mean.ceo](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=5743)

**Creator headcount (definitions conflict)**
- ~207M content creators worldwide (most-repeated figure, attributed to Statista via roundups). — [Scrumball](https://scrumball.com/blog/how-many-content-creators-worldwide); [Inbeat](https://inbeat.agency/blog/creator-economy-statistics)
- Linktree (2023 data): 200M active creators, of whom 2M+ are "expert-level". — [Inbeat](https://inbeat.agency/blog/creator-economy-statistics)
- Goldman (2023): 50M people consider themselves creators. — [Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/the-creator-economy-could-approach-half-a-trillion-dollars-by-2027)
- MBO Partners (2024): 162M US creators, 45M of them "professional". Sources disagree on whether this is US or global. — [Fungies](https://fungies.io/?p=40731); contradicted in scope by [logie.ai](https://logie.ai/news/?p=1753) (presents it as global)
- ~45–47% of creators self-identify as full-time (DemandSage 46.7%; IMH 44.9%). These are survey samples skewed to engaged creators. — [Scrumball](https://scrumball.com/blog/how-many-content-creators-worldwide)
- US full-time digital creator roles rose from 200k to 1.5M between 2020 and 2024 (estimate). — [logie.ai](https://logie.ai/news/?p=1753)

**Advertiser money (brand-deal pool)**
- IAB (Nov 20, 2025): US creator ad spend of $13.9B (2021) → $29.5B (2024) → $37B (2025F, +26% YoY, ~4x faster than total media) → ~$44B (2026F). 48% of advertisers call creators a "must buy". The survey covered 450+ US ad decision-makers. — [IAB](https://iab.com/news/creator-economy-ad-spend-to-reach-37-billion-in-2025-growing-4x-faster-than-total-media-industry-according-to-iab)
- Growth is decelerating: +34% in 2023→24 vs +26% in 2025. — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/11/20/iab-annual-creator-economy-report-us-2025/)

**Platform payouts (what creators actually receive)**
- YouTube: >$100B paid to creators, artists and media companies over the four years 2022–2025 (announced at Made on YouTube, Sept 2025). That includes ads, Premium/Music subscriptions and other YPP features. Creators get 55% of YPP ad and subscription revenue. Channels earning >$100k rose 45% YoY, attributed to TV-screen viewing. — [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/145986/youtube-claim-100-billion-dollar-pay-out); [Mezha](https://mezha.media/en/news/youtube-paid-100-billion-to-creators-since-2021-304893). Some outlets date the window from 2021, so the start date is in conflict.
- YouTube (Feb 2025 CEO letter): >$70B paid over the prior three years. — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-february-letter-neal-mohan-partner-program/)
- Patreon: >$10B cumulative creator payouts as of Aug 2025, >$2B/yr now, 25M+ paid memberships, annual creator earnings up ~150% vs 2020. That compares with $3.5B cumulative in Dec 2021. Podcasts alone earned $472M in 2024. — [Axios (Aug 5, 2025)](https://www.axios.com/2025/08/05/patreon-10-billion-creator-economy-ai). Third-party tracker Graphtreon sees only ~$24M/mo on public pages, far below company claims. — [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/report/patreon)
- OnlyFans FY2025 (year to Nov 30, 2025): ~$6.3B to creators (~20% of ~$30B cumulative), net revenue ~$1.55B (+10%), 2.5M active creators, 132M fan accounts. — [Tubefilter (Aug 26, 2026)](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/26/onlyfans-million-dollar-earners-keily-blair-earnings/amp/). $25B paid since 2016 (CEO, Oct 2025). — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-21/onlyfans-has-paid-creators-25-billion-since-2016-ceo-says). Earlier baseline: ~$6.6B gross and $5.32B to creators in FY2023. — [Techleap](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/note/onlyfans-reports-6-6b-in-gross-revenues); [SAN](https://san.com/?p=230572)
- TikTok: total creator earnings in 2025 were an estimated ~$15.5B across payouts, brand deals, LIVE gifts and Shop commissions, +28% vs 2024. This is a third-party model with no disclosed methodology. — [TT Calculator](https://ttcalculator.net/data/earnings/tiktok-creator-economy-size/)

**Regional sizes (relevant to launch geographies)**
- India (BCG, May 3, 2025, WAVES summit): 2–2.5M active creators (>1k followers), of whom only ~8–10% monetize. Creators influence $350–400B of consumer spend, projected to exceed $1T by 2030. Direct ecosystem revenue is $20–25B now, projected at $100–125B by 2030. — [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/india-from-content-to-commerce-mapping-indias-creator-economy); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/how-it-works/indias-creator-economy-influences-400-bn-in-consumer-spend-set-to-drive-1-tn-by-2030-bcg-64390.htm); [Kotak MF](https://www.kotakmf.com/Information/blogs/inside-india-creator-economy_)
- Africa: the digital creator economy was $3.08B in 2023, projected at $17.84B by 2030 (28.5% CAGR), as cited in Nigeria's Creator Economy Report 2025. — [Condia](https://thecondia.com/nigeria-creator-economy-31-2m-report-communique/)
- Nigeria: the inaugural Nigeria Creator Economy Report 2025 (with the federal ministry and NCAC) sizes the sector at ~$31.2M. The definition looks narrow, since it is below Nigerian artists' Spotify royalties alone. — [Condia](https://thecondia.com/nigeria-creator-economy-31-2m-report-communique/)
- Kenya: influencers earned an estimated Ksh 1.07B in 2025 (OdipoDev, *Decoding Kenya's Creator Economy*). — [HapaKenya (Apr 10, 2026)](https://hapakenya.com/2026/04/10/kenyan-creators-earned-ksh-1-07b-in-2025-according-to-odipo-dev-report); [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/technology/top-influencers-earn-sh296m-as-creator-economy-tops-sh1bn-5415606)
- Brazil: 14M+ digital content creators (Wake Creators, July 2025), or ~6.7% of the population (Statista/Scopen). — [O Povo](https://mais.opovo.com.br/jornal/reportagem/2025/07/22/a-nova-economia-da-influencia-cresce-no-brasil.html); [Meio & Mensagem](https://www.meioemensagem.com.br/midia/qual-e-o-perfil-dos-criadores-de-conteudo-brasileiros). Contradicted by a Nielsen-attributed figure of 500k+ creators (likely older, narrower definition). — [ABC da Comunicação](https://www.abcdacomunicacao.com.br/qual-e-o-perfil-do-influenciador-brasileiro-estudo-inedito-da-squid-traca-o-raio-x-dos-criadores-de-conteudo/)

### Inferences
- For a pitch, the defensible "why now" money anchors are hard flows: IAB $37B (2025) → $44B (2026) US brand spend; YouTube ~$25B/yr average payouts (from $100B over four years); OnlyFans $6.3B; Patreon ~$2B. The $480B Goldman figure is a 2023 forecast and should be labeled as such.
- Kenya's Ksh 1.07B is roughly US$8M at ~129 KES/USD (my arithmetic). Nigeria's $31.2M is similarly tiny. Measured *formal* creator income in African markets is very small next to audience size. That supports the thesis that audience is abundant and monetization rails are missing.
- Brand-deal money is growing but concentrating (see Q2). New fan-paid revenue lines are where incremental creator income must come from.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the Goldman 2025 update, the IMH "State of the Creator Economy 2025/2026", SignalFire or Linktree 2025 primary documents (fetch failures). I found no verified 2025/2026 global creator headcount.
- YouTube does not break out fan-funding (Super Chat, memberships) totals.
- Secondary reports cite FY2024 OnlyFans figures of $7.22B gross and $5.8B to creators. I could not tie these to a specific retrieved article, so verify them against Fenix International filings.

## 2. The monetization gap: who earns meaningful income, by tier, and why large audiences under-monetize

### Takeaway
Across every survey, most creators earn little: roughly half or more make under $15k/yr, and in emerging markets most make under $100/month. Income follows a power law (e.g., Kenya's top 10 influencers capture ~30% of the market). Mid-to-large creators under-monetize because income depends on brand deals (lumpy, gatekept, about 70% of revenue), platform take rates are 30–55%, algorithmic reach is volatile, and in Africa and South Asia native monetization programs are often unavailable.

### Cited Findings
**Income distribution (global / US-skewed surveys)**
- IMH × NeoReach Creator Earnings Report 2025: more than half of creators earn under $15k/yr, vs ~48% in 2023. It surveyed 3,000+ creators with a combined following of 1.1B+. 43.8% of respondents had <100k followers. IMH notes income tends to accelerate past a ~$15k/yr threshold. — [IMH](https://influencermarketinghub.com/creator-earnings-report-2025/); [Hopp](https://www.hopp.co/post/creator-economy-statistics); [Net Influencer / NeoReach](https://www.netinfluencer.com/over-half-of-full-time-creators-earn-below-us-living-wage-despite-250b-industry-neoreach-finds/) (over half of *full-time* creators earn below a US living wage)
- NeoReach: income rises with tenure, and ~80% of year-four creators clear $10k. — [Uscreen](https://www.uscreen.tv/blog/creator-economy-statistics/)
- Linktree 2023: only about half of creators on Linktree make any money. Of those who do, 72% earn <$500 and 2% earn >$50k. — [Passionfru](https://passionfru.it/linktree-creators-109807/)
- Linktree 2022: 12% of full-time creators earn >$50k and 46% earn <$1k. Among part-time creators, 3% earn >$50k and 68% earn <$1k. — [Techdirt](https://www.techdirt.com/tag/creator-report/); [Net Influencer](https://netinfluencer.com/2022-creator-report/)
- Linktree 2024 Creator Commerce Report (~2,000 creators): 70% earned <$49k in the past year. — [SmartCompany](https://smartcompany.com.au/finance/stream-of-revenue-online-creators-linktree-report)
- Goldman: ~4% of creators earn >$100k/yr. — [Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/the-creator-economy-could-approach-half-a-trillion-dollars-by-2027)
- CreatorIQ: creator pay skews to the top tier despite rising influencer budgets. — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/creatoriq-survey-finds-creator-pay-skews-to-top-tier-despite-rising-influencer-marketing-spend/); also [Creator Handbook: "widening monetization gap"](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/study-finds-creator-economy-is-growing-with-a-widening-monetization-gap/)
- Third-party TikTok estimate (2025): median monetizing creator ~$150/month, ~67% of monetized creators under $100/month, top 5% at $5k+/month. — [TT Calculator](https://ttcalculator.net/data/earnings/tiktok-creator-economy-size/) (methodology undisclosed)
- Twitch: median Affiliate payout ~$28.50/month (SullyGnome via roundup, unverified). The claim that the top 1% take ~72% of payouts is attributed to The Verge but unverified. — [Voxbooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/twitch-affiliate-statistics-2026)
- Twitch says "over one million streamers earn money" each month (early 2024). — [Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/twitch-makes-changes-to-streamer-payouts/)

**By follower tier**
- I found no 2025 survey reporting median annual income by follower tier. Only per-post rate estimates exist. Micro (10k–100k) Instagram sponsored posts are ~$100–500/post in one estimate and ~$300–1,000/post in another. — [Stack Influence](https://stackinfluence.com/?p=350117); [Podcastle](https://podcastle.ai/blog/how-much-do-influencers-make)
- Platform CPMs vary ~100x. YouTube is ~$2–25 per 1k views, TikTok ~$0.40–1.00, and Instagram ~$0.01–0.09 (blog estimates). Platform matters as much as follower count. — [Podcastle](https://podcastle.ai/blog/how-much-do-influencers-make)

**Emerging markets (launch-relevant)**
- Africa (2026 analysis reported by Yournotify): 6 in 10 surveyed African creators earn <US$100/month. Brand sponsorships are the largest revenue source at 28%, with digital products at 25%. — [Yournotify](https://yournotify.com/blog/how-nigerian-creators-can-earn-beyond-brand-deals/)
- Nigeria (CcHUB, Sept 2026, faith-content creators): >70% have yet to earn any online revenue. — [TechEconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/nigerias-faith-creator-economy-is-growing-but-70-earn-no-online-revenue-report)
- Nigeria: four local creator-commerce startups paid >₦22B (~$15.7M) to creators over the past year, with Selar at ₦18B (~$12.86M) in 2025. An earlier version of the same article gave lower numbers. — [Techpoint Africa](https://techpoint.africa/insight/nigerias-creator-economy-may-finally-be-coming-of-age/)
- Kenya (OdipoDev 2025): the top 10 influencers earned Ksh 296.2M, ~30% of the Ksh 1.07B total, and the top 20 earned ~40%. The leader, comedian Eric Omondi, made ~Ksh 57M. SMEs anchor ~80% of creator partnerships, and top sponsored posts reach ~Ksh 500k. Instagram converts best: top creators turned 40.8% of views into paid content. TikTok shows a reach-to-revenue gap. — [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/technology/top-influencers-earn-sh296m-as-creator-economy-tops-sh1bn-5415606); [Capital FM](https://www.capitalfm.co.ke/business/2026/04/eric-omondi-tops-kenya-influencers-with-sh57mn-earnings/); [HapaKenya](https://hapakenya.com/2026/04/10/kenyan-creators-earned-ksh-1-07b-in-2025-according-to-odipo-dev-report)
- India (BCG 2025): only ~8–10% of 2–2.5M active creators monetize. Most nano and micro creators earn <₹18,000/month (~$205). — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/how-it-works/indias-creator-economy-influences-400-bn-in-consumer-spend-set-to-drive-1-tn-by-2030-bcg-64390.htm); [Kotak MF](https://www.kotakmf.com/Information/blogs/inside-india-creator-economy_)
- Brazil (YouPix, Oct 2025): 1 in 2 creators with 3+ years' experience earns >R$10k/month, and half live exclusively from digital influence. — [Señal News](https://senalnews.com/es/data/brasil-madura-el-mercado-de-influencers-y-genera-ingresos-a-creadores)

**Why big audiences under-monetize: revenue sources and their limits**
- *Brand-deal dependency.* Brand deals are ~70% of creator revenue (Goldman survey). — [Goldman Sachs](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/the-creator-economy-could-approach-half-a-trillion-dollars-by-2027). **Conflict:** Linktree's first Creator Report found brand partnerships were only ~10% of income for most creators, and ~70% of creators earn only a fraction of income from them. — [SmartCompany](https://smartcompany.com.au/finance/stream-of-revenue-online-creators-linktree-report). The samples differ: Goldman's skews to professionals, Linktree's to its long-tail user base.
- Brand deals are gatekept by trust and fit: 63% of creators avoid brand deals with companies they don't believe in (Linktree 2024). — [SmartCompany](https://smartcompany.com.au/finance/stream-of-revenue-online-creators-linktree-report). In Kenya, limited platform payouts force reliance on brand deals. — [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/technology/top-influencers-earn-sh296m-as-creator-economy-tops-sh1bn-5415606)
- *Income mix is diversifying.* One 2025 survey has live streaming at 32% and ad revenue at 29% as leading income sources. — [Net Influencer (Creator Lightning survey)](https://www.netinfluencer.com/creator-economy-split-reflects-divergent-income-models-creator-lightning-survey/). Epidemic Sound 2025 (3,000 US/UK creators): 27% say fan subscriptions are a main channel, and 23% say merch. — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/report-95-percent-of-creators-embrace-direct-to-fan-models-as-creator-economy-matures)
- *Platform take rates:*
  - YouTube keeps ~45% of YPP ad and subscription revenue; creators get 55%. — [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/145986/youtube-claim-100-billion-dollar-pay-out)
  - YouTube Super Chat gives creators 70%. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/pt/blog/post/youtube-super-chat-guide)
  - Twitch subs split 50/50 by default, with 60/40 and 70/30 tiers via Partner Plus. The $100k cap on 70/30 was removed in 2024. Bits pay $0.01 per bit against a $1.40 price per 100, so creators get ~71%. — [Insider Gaming](https://insider-gaming.com/twitch-makes-changes-to-streamer-payouts/)
  - TikTok retains ~50% of LIVE gift value at cash-out. — [Nuelink](https://blog.nuelink.com/tiktok-coins/)
  - OnlyFans keeps ~20%: FY2023 $5.32B of $6.6B gross went to creators. — [SAN](https://san.com/?p=230572)
  - WhatsApp Channel subscriptions: Meta reportedly will eventually take 10% (single source). — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/whatsapp-introduces-ads-creator-monetization-tools-first-time)
- *Access gaps in emerging markets:*
  - TikTok's Creator Rewards Programme hasn't fully rolled out in Africa. African creators rely on LIVE gifts, subscriptions and "Work With Artist" campaigns. — [Capital FM Kenya](https://www.capitalfm.co.ke/thesauce/inside-tiktok-monetisation-how-creators-in-kenya-are-earning-from-their-content)
  - A commentator claims TikTok's in-app creator fund runs in only ~14 countries, excluding Nigeria (unverified personal post). — [LinkedIn](https://gh.linkedin.com/in/jesse-wallace-bb449a3b)
- *Algorithm volatility:*
  - 53% of creators say reaching followers is harder than five years ago (Patreon State of Create, 2025). — [Nieman Lab](https://niemanlab.org/reading/survey-more-than-half-of-creators-say-its-harder-to-reach-their-followers-today-than-five-years-ago)
  - ~70% report unpredictable monthly income on major platforms, and 75% say platforms punish creators who don't post constantly. 78% say algorithms affect what they create, and 56% say algorithms keep them from creative interests. — [Net Influencer (2025 survey coverage)](https://www.netinfluencer.com/?p=36098) (attribution between the Epidemic Sound and Patreon studies is uncertain)
  - 59% of TikTok creators and 52% of commerce creators cite algorithm unpredictability as a major frustration (URLgenius, Apr 2025, n=121, mostly >100k followers). — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/?p=33329)

### Inferences
- The sweet spot is creators with 10k–500k followers and high engagement. They have real attention but fall below the threshold for steady brand deals, especially in markets where platform payout programs are absent (Nigeria, much of Africa). A prediction-market fee is a non-brand, non-algorithm-dependent revenue line, which is exactly what surveys say creators lack.
- Take-rate positioning: if creator fees come from trading volume and are paid instantly in stablecoins, the pitch is "your fee per trade, no 30–55% platform cut, no payout-program eligibility". The real limit is fan trading volume, not platform share.
- Power-law concentration means a small number of "anchor creators" per market (e.g., the top 20 in Kenya) will drive most early volume. Seeding with a handful of high-engagement mid-tier creators per vertical is more realistic than broad long-tail onboarding.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable 2025 income-by-follower-tier distribution (nano/micro/mid/macro/mega). IMH's tier breakdown exists, but its charts were not retrievable.
- I found no data on what share of income 100k–1M follower creators in Nigeria, Kenya or Brazil get from each source.

## 3. Fan engagement and willingness to pay

### Takeaway
Paid fan engagement is large and proven: OnlyFans has 132M fan accounts and $6.3B paid out, Patreon has 25M+ paid memberships, 68% of TikTok users have tried LIVE gifting, and over a million Twitch streamers earn monthly. A ~15–20% "superfan" segment spends ~80% more than average. The closest behavioral analog to this product is Twitch Predictions, where millions of viewers each week stake (non-cash) channel points on streamer-defined outcomes. Free-to-play predictor games and reality-TV voting (BBNaija All-Stars drew 1.53B votes) show mass appetite for "call the outcome" mechanics.

### Cited Findings
**Superfans**
- Luminate: ~20% of US music listeners are superfans, and they spend ~80% more per month on music than the average listener. — [Luminate](https://luminatedata.com/?p=2905)
- 15% of the US general population are superfans (headline). — [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/15-of-the-general-population-of-the-us-are-superfans-heres-what-that-means-for-the-music-business)
- Goldman Sachs Music in the Air 2025: superfan monetization could add ~$4.3B a year (2026 basis). Another write-up says up to $6.6B (**conflict, unreconciled**). Global music is projected at $104.9B (2024) → $196.8B (2035). — [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/emerging-markets-superfans-and-price-rises-7-takeaways-from-goldman-sachs-new-music-in-the-air-report)
- Goldman 2023 scenario: if 20% of subscribers are superfans spending 2x, that is a $4.2B opportunity. This is a hypothetical multiplier, not measured willingness to pay. — [Fortune (2023)](https://fortune.com/2023/07/11/music-streamers-apple-spotify-should-increase-price-goldman-sachs-says)
- A skeptical view: recorded-music revenue missed Goldman's 2024 expectations, prompting downgrades. — [The Quietus](https://thequietus.com/?p=260100)

**Paid fan engagement products**
- TikTok LIVE (Ipsos study commissioned by TikTok, June–July 2025, n=500, ages 18–49): 68% of TikTok users have tried gifting on LIVE, and 50% are likely to gift in the next month. 60,000+ US LIVE creators are projected to earn more than the median part-time monthly income from gifts in 2025. — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=189710). Third-party estimates: ~$6B global TikTok in-app spend in 2024, mostly coins for gifts, and >$11M/day in tipping. — [Nuelink](https://blog.nuelink.com/tiktok-coins/) (low-confidence blog estimates)
- YouTube Super Chat (older, ~2019): 90,000+ channels had received Super Chats, and ~20,000 ranked it as their top revenue stream. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/live-from-vidcon-creating-new/). YouTube memberships grew 50%+ YoY as of early 2024. — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/youtube-february-letter-neal-mohan-partner-program/) (attribution to a specific letter is uncertain)
- Twitch Predictions (launched late 2020) is the direct analog. Streamers define outcomes, and viewers stake channel points (no cash value) during a 1–30 minute window, up to 250k points. Winners split the pool pro rata. Unresolved predictions are refunded after 24 hours. Twitch reported "millions of viewers each week" participating, and team badges spur rivalry. Predictions have been available via API/EventSub since May 2021. — [Tubefilter (2020)](https://www.tubefilter.com/2020/11/13/twitch-launches-predictions-feature-channel-points/); [Twitch Blog (May 2021)](https://blog.twitch.tv/en/2021/05/24/polls-and-channel-points-predictions-have-leveled-up-with-twitch-api-and-eventsub-support). In some countries viewers are restricted to "viewer mode" (no winnings) for legal reasons. — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/twitch-predictions/)

- Willingness to pay is concentrated in a ~15–20% superfan core. Sizing should assume a small share of followers trading, consistent with Socios' ~5% conversion and TikTok gifting skew. Design for high per-fan engagement, not broad conversion.
- Story-native interaction (polls, quizzes) already sees outsized engagement. A YES/NO card shared to WhatsApp Status or IG Stories fits existing fan habits.

### Inferences
- Priority verticals for launch, ranked by question frequency, resolvability and fan tribalism:
  1. Football fan pages and sports commentators in Nigeria, Kenya and Ghana.
  2. Gaming and IRL streamers.
  3. BBNaija and reality-TV commentators, which are seasonal: July–October.
  4. Afrobeats and music stan accounts.
  5. Crypto KOLs, who are crypto-native but carry the highest reputational and regulatory baggage.
- **Critical design implication: insider trading.** In a model where creators make markets about *their own content*, the creator and team know the outcome. The MrBeast case shows regulators and the press treat this as insider trading. Markets should resolve on public, externally verifiable outcomes (match results, chart data, eviction results). Alternatively, creators and their teams should be blocked from trading their own markets, with self-resolved markets flagged as such.
- Creator consent matters: QTCinderella's cease-and-desist shows creators resent third-party markets about them. A creator-*led*, opt-in model is a positive differentiator vs Kalshi and Polymarket.
- Distribution risk: X restricted paid gambling promos (Feb 2026), and the Polymarket fake-bet scandal will invite scrutiny of creator-promoted betting. Disclosure ("creator earns fees from this market") and real-trade-only displays should be built in.

## 6. Crypto-native creator monetization 2024–2026: creator coins, Pump.fun streamers, SocialFi lessons, Solana tooling

### Takeaway
Crypto creator monetization repeatedly produces explosive but short-lived spikes. Zora creator coins reached hundreds of millions in volume in 2025, then saw quarterly revenue fall ~98% by Q2 2026. Base App ended creator rewards. Pump.fun's creator fees paid millions per day in Sept 2025, but platform fees fell ~75% YoY by Jan 2026. friend.tech went from 10k+ DAU to dormant within about a year. The durable lesson is that trading-fee revenue built on speculative token value is fragile. Fees anchored to recurring, outcome-based engagement (markets that resolve) have a different, potentially more sustainable, dynamic, but that is unproven.

**Zora / Base (creator coins)**

### Inferences
- Benchmark creator-fee levels: Zora 1%, Bags 1%, Believe 70% of a ~1% fee, and Pump.fun 0.05–0.95% scaled by market cap. Creators and fans already accept a ~1%-of-volume creator fee as normal in Solana and Base creator tooling.
- Differentiate from creator coins. Prediction markets resolve, so a creator can run a new market every match, episode or stream. Fee income comes from a repeatable engagement cadence, not from an ever-rising token price that inevitably collapses (Zora -98%, friend.tech, FRIEND -99%). The pitch is "a fresh game every week, not a token that has to pump".
- The Pump.fun experience warns that very high creator incentives drive low-quality or extreme content and farming. Creator fees should scale with *real fan volume*, with anti-wash-trading controls, and should not reward market creation itself.
- Viral, feed-integrated distribution (Base App feed → Zora token creation up ~8x) drives step-change adoption. That supports the WhatsApp Status / IG Story share-card strategy.

## 7. What creators say they want (surveys)

### Takeaway
Creators consistently say they want direct, owned relationships with fans and predictable recurring income that doesn't depend on algorithms or brand deals. 95% already reach audiences directly, about 70% report unpredictable monthly income, and 53% find it harder to reach followers than five years ago. They also want instant payouts and tools that deepen fan engagement. Most of these surveys are sponsored by direct-to-fan tool vendors, so treat the exact percentages loosely.

### Cited Findings
- Epidemic Sound "Future of the Creator Economy 2025" (3,000 US/UK creators): 95% reach audiences directly, 27% say fan subscriptions are a main channel, and 23% say merch. The shift is attributed to platform saturation and algorithm changes disrupting ad income. — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/report-95-percent-of-creators-embrace-direct-to-fan-models-as-creator-economy-matures)
- Patreon State of Create (Feb 2025; NewtonX; 1,000+ creators, 2,000 fans): creators increasingly judge success by direct audience relationships, discovery and steady monetization rather than follower counts. 53% say reaching followers is harder than five years ago. — [Hypebot](https://www.hypebot.com/state-of-create-report-on-patreon-creator-income.md); [Nieman Lab](https://niemanlab.org/reading/survey-more-than-half-of-creators-say-its-harder-to-reach-their-followers-today-than-five-years-ago)
- ~70% report unpredictable monthly income, 75% feel punished if not constantly publishing, 78% say algorithms shape what they create, and 56% say algorithms block their creative interests (2025 survey coverage; attribution uncertain). — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/?p=36098)
- URLgenius (Apr 2025, n=121): 59% of TikTok creators cite algorithm unpredictability. 47% of TikTok creators were "very concerned" about a TikTok ban vs 7% of commerce creators, and 99% of commerce creators use affiliate income as a buffer. — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/?p=33329)
- LTK's CEO: algorithms have overtaken follower counts as the driver of reach, so an existing audience no longer guarantees visibility. — [Global Dating Insights](https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/featured/social-media-followers-have-never-mattered-less-for-creators)
- Patreon (late 2021, older): ~70% of creators felt social platforms put them at a disadvantage. — [Axios (2022)](https://www.axios.com/2022/10/25/survey-patreon-creators-big-tech-social-media)
- Linktree 2024: 63% avoid brand deals they don't trust, and trust is "persuasion currency". — [SmartCompany](https://smartcompany.com.au/finance/stream-of-revenue-online-creators-linktree-report)
- Brand deals still matter: 86% of 500+ surveyed creators work with brands monthly (2025 playbook). — [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/?p=36098)
- Visa (2025 Creator Economy Report, via secondary): 57% prefer digital payouts for instant access to funds. — [Corepay](https://corepay.net/articles/visa-direct-stablecoin-payouts-what-it-means-for-merchants-gig-platforms-and-high-risk-businesses/) (unverified)
- African creators: limited direct platform earnings force reliance on brand deals. Payment infrastructure, not influence, determines profitability. — [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/technology/top-influencers-earn-sh296m-as-creator-economy-tops-sh1bn-5415606); [TechCabal (ACER 2026)](https://techcabal.com/2026/02/05/africa-creator-economy-report/)
- Creators object to *non-consensual* markets about them (QTCinderella cease-and-desist over Kalshi and Polymarket Streamer Awards markets). — [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/the-game-awards-2025-polymarket-kalshi-gambling.md)

### Inferences
- Positioning that maps to stated creator needs:
  1. "Recurring income every match, episode or stream" answers income unpredictability.
  2. "Revenue from your audience, not the algorithm or brands" answers algorithm and brand dependency.
  3. "Instant USD-stablecoin payouts" answers the payout preference and FX volatility in Nigeria and Kenya.
  4. "You control the markets about you" answers the consent problem with Kalshi and Polymarket.
  5. "Engagement tool that boosts Story and Status interaction" answers the reach decline.
- The trust and authenticity concern (63% avoid untrusted brands) plus the Polymarket fake-bet scandal mean creators will weigh reputational risk. Clear disclosure and fan-protection features (limits, no fake wins) will matter for recruiting reputable mid-tier creators.

### Gaps
- I found no survey asking creators specifically about prediction markets, polls-for-money or betting-adjacent monetization, nor about crypto or stablecoin payout preferences in Nigeria, Kenya or Brazil.
- The Patreon State of Create fan-side findings (2,000 fans: willingness to pay, what fans want) were not retrievable in detail.
